🔥 Warm-Up 0/4
✓
Stationary Bike 10 min · Easy Pace ▼
How to do it:
  1. Adjust the seat so your knee has a slight bend at the bottom of each pedal stroke.
  2. Set resistance to a light level — you should be able to hold a conversation.
  3. Sit upright with a straight spine; avoid hunching forward.
  4. Pedal at a comfortable, steady pace for 10 minutes.
Loosens lower back joints, increases blood flow to the discs, and prepares your spine for movement without compression.

Cat Stretch 2 sets · 10 reps ▼
How to do it:
  1. Get on all fours — hands under shoulders, knees under hips. If kneeling bothers your knees, place a folded towel or mat pad under them.
  2. Exhale and round your spine up toward the ceiling, tucking your chin and pelvis — this is the "Cat" portion.
  3. Slowly return to a flat, neutral back. Do NOT drop your belly or arch your lower back into extension (the "Cow" portion) — stop at neutral.
  4. Repeat for 10 slow reps, moving only between rounded and neutral. Never force the range of motion.
We dropped the Cow (extension/arching) half since it reproduced your pain — that motion narrows the same facet joints and nerve openings your MRI already flagged as tight (facet arthritis, foraminal narrowing). The Cat (rounding/flexion) half alone still safely mobilizes the arthritic joints.

Deep Core Activation 2 sets · 10 reps ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor. Place your fingertips just inside your hip bones (the bony bumps at the front of your pelvis).
  2. Take a slow breath in through your nose.
  3. As you exhale slowly, gently draw your lower abdomen inward — as if bracing for a light poke in the stomach. Do NOT suck your stomach in hard or hold your breath.
  4. You should feel a subtle, deep tightening under your fingertips. That is your transverse abdominis — your deep core "corset" muscle — switching on.
  5. Hold the gentle brace for 5–6 seconds while continuing to breathe normally. Release fully. That is one rep. Do 10 reps.
Most back pain sufferers have a "turned off" deep core after months of guarding. This exercise wakes it back up. Your machine core work only works properly if this foundation is already activated. Takes 2 minutes and pays dividends the entire workout. Focus on the internal sensation — this is quiet, internal work, not a visible movement.

✓
Ski Erg 5 min · Easy Pace ▼
How to do it:
  1. Stand facing the machine, feet shoulder-width apart, holding a handle in each hand at a comfortable overhead height — don't force a full overhead reach if your shoulder feels tight.
  2. Hinge gently at the hips and pull both handles down and back past your hips, like a poling motion in cross-country skiing.
  3. Let the handles return smoothly overhead and repeat.
  4. Keep the hip hinge light and your lower back neutral — don't round at the bottom of each pull.
  5. Work at an easy pace for 5 minutes.
Standing, low-impact, and works your lats, core, and legs together with zero floor work or spinal compression. Keep the overhead reach comfortable and the hip hinge gentle.

🧘 Mobility & Stretching 0/3
✓
Couch Stretch 30–60 sec · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Kneel in front of a couch, bench, or wall with one knee down and your back shin resting up against it (foot pointing up).
  2. Bring your front foot flat on the floor in front of you, knee bent to about 90°.
  3. Come up tall through your torso, then gently lean slightly away from the back leg — this opens more space between your ribs and hip instead of arching your lower back.
  4. Squeeze the glute on the back-leg side lightly to deepen the stretch through the front of that hip and thigh.
  5. Hold 30–60 seconds, breathing normally, then switch sides.
  6. If kneeling is uncomfortable, pad the knee well or use the standing hip flexor stretch instead.
A deeper hip flexor/quad stretch than the standard kneeling version — your PT's pick for where your hip flexor tightness is currently at. The lean-away cue keeps the stretch in the hip instead of your lower back.

Figure-4 Piriformis Stretch 30 sec · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent.
  2. Cross your right ankle over your left knee (figure-4 shape).
  3. Reach through and pull your left thigh toward your chest until you feel a stretch in the right glute/hip.
  4. Hold 30 seconds, switch sides.
A tight piriformis can compress the sciatic nerve and worsen back pain. Often one of the most immediately relieving stretches.

✓
Hip Flexor Stretch (Kneeling) 30 sec · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Kneel on one knee on a mat (folded towel under knee — use extra padding if your knees are sensitive).
  2. Place other foot flat on floor in front, making a 90° angle.
  3. Push hips forward gently until you feel a stretch in the front of the back hip.
  4. Hold 30 seconds. Keep spine upright — don't arch your lower back. Switch sides.
  5. Knee-friendly alternative: Stand and place your back foot up on a bench behind you instead of kneeling, then gently press hips forward.
Tight hip flexors pull the pelvis forward and increase lumbar compression. Stretching them daily is one of the highest-impact things you can do. Use the standing version if kneeling irritates your knees.

💪 Core & Stability (Machine-Based) 0/4
✓
Opposition Crunch 10 slow exhales ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent. Hold a stability ball (or a firm pillow) between your hands and your knees, arms and thighs pressing toward each other around it.
  2. Take a deep breath in.
  3. As you exhale slowly through pursed lips, push your knees and hands into the ball at the same time — this creates tension through your core without moving your spine.
  4. While exhaling, curl your shoulders just slightly up toward the ball and hold until your exhale is complete.
  5. Rest, take a couple of relaxed breaths, and repeat for 10 total exhales.
Your PT's replacement for the crunch machine — same "ab-engaging" feeling, but the ball creates the tension so your spine barely moves, instead of loading it with resisted flexion.

✓
Pallof Press 2–3 sets · 10–12 reps · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. At a cable machine, set a D-handle at chest height.
  2. Stand sideways to the machine, feet shoulder-width apart. Grab the handle with both hands and hold it at your chest, close to your sternum.
  3. Step far enough away from the machine that there is light tension on the cable.
  4. Brace your deep core (like you practiced in Deep Core Activation). Keep your hips square — do not let the cable pull you sideways or rotate your torso.
  5. Slowly press your hands straight out in front of you until your arms are fully extended. Pause 1–2 seconds — feel your core resisting the rotation.
  6. Slowly return your hands to your chest. That is one rep. Complete all reps on one side, then turn around and repeat facing the other direction.
Trains your core to resist rotation — protects your lumbar spine during everyday twisting (turning to reverse a car, reaching sideways, getting out of bed). Start very light — the challenge is staying perfectly still, not the weight.

Pallof to Warrior Press 2–3 sets · 8–10 reps · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. At a cable machine, set a D-handle at chest height.
  2. Stand sideways to the machine, feet shoulder-width apart. Grab the handle with both hands and hold it at your chest, close to your sternum.
  3. Step far enough away from the machine that there is light tension on the cable.
  4. Brace your deep core. Press your hands straight out until arms are extended, keeping hips square.
  5. From there, slowly raise both hands overhead only as high as is comfortable for your shoulder, resisting the urge to arch your lower back as your arms go up.
  6. Lower back to chest height with control. That is one rep. Complete all reps on one side, then turn around and repeat facing the other direction.
Where the standard Pallof Press trains your core to resist side rotation, the overhead reach here trains it to resist extension — keeping your ribs from flaring up and your lower back from arching as your arms go overhead. Keep the range small and pain-free given your shoulder arthritis.

✓
Dead Bug 2–3 sets · 8 reps · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back, arms reaching straight up toward the ceiling, knees bent to 90° directly over your hips (tabletop position).
  2. Gently brace your core (like Deep Core Activation) and press your lower back lightly into the floor — hold that the whole set.
  3. Slowly lower your right arm overhead and your left leg straight out toward the floor at the same time, only as far as you can go without your lower back arching up off the floor.
  4. Pause, then slowly return to the starting position under control.
  5. Repeat with the opposite arm and leg. That's one rep per side — continue alternating for 8 reps each side.
Trains your core to keep your spine still while your limbs move — the same coordination that protects your back during everyday activities. If your lower back arches off the floor, that's the signal to shorten the range until it stays flat.

🦵 Lower Body Strength (Spine-Friendly) 0/5
✓
Leg Press Machine 3 sets · 12 reps · Light weight ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit in the machine with back fully supported against the pad.
  2. Place feet shoulder-width apart on the platform.
  3. Lower platform until knees are around 90° — don't let knees cave inward.
  4. Push through heels to extend (don't lock out knees at the top).
  5. Start with very light weight — not a max-effort exercise.
Builds quads, glutes, and hamstrings WITHOUT loading your spine — unlike squats or deadlifts. The safest lower body machine for back conditions.

✓
Glute Bridge 3 sets · 12–15 reps ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back on a mat, knees bent, feet flat on the floor about hip-width apart, arms relaxed at your sides.
  2. Gently brace your core (like Deep Core Activation), then squeeze your glutes to lift your hips off the floor.
  3. Lift only until your body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees — do NOT arch or overextend your lower back at the top. Think "tuck," not "arch."
  4. Squeeze at the top for 1–2 seconds, then lower slowly and under control.
Builds the glutes, which are a key stabilizer for the pelvis and take load off the lower back. Keep the lift driven by your glutes, not by arching your spine — stop the range right where your body is level, since going higher extends the lower back the same way the retired back extension machine did.

How to do it:

Leg Extension Machine Workout A 3 sets · 12 reps · Light-moderate ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit in the machine with your knees aligned to the machine's pivot point and shins behind the lower pad.

Suitcase Carry Workout B 3 sets · 30–40 sec · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Hold a single dumbbell or kettlebell at your side, like a suitcase, feet hip-width apart.
  2. Gently brace your core and stand tall — do not lean or side-bend toward the weight.
  3. Walk 20–30 steps (about 30–40 seconds), keeping your torso upright and level the whole time.
  4. Switch the weight to your other hand and repeat.
An excellent anti-lateral-flexion core exercise — your obliques and deep trunk muscles work hard to keep you upright while you walk, with zero spinal flexion, rotation, or floor work. Start light; the goal is a level torso, not how much weight you can carry.

🏋️ Upper Body (Spinal Decompression) 0/2
✓
Chest Press Machine 3 sets · 12 reps · Light-moderate ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit with your back flat against the pad, handles at chest height.
  2. Set a light-moderate weight.
  3. Press the handles forward until your arms are extended but not locked out.
  4. Slowly return to start, keeping shoulder blades lightly retracted — don't let shoulders roll forward at the bottom.
  5. Stop the movement early if you feel any shoulder pinching.
A machine chest press keeps the movement path fixed and supported, unlike a free-weight bench press — much easier on an arthritic shoulder since there's nothing to stabilize. Keep the weight light and stop the range before any pinch or ache.

Chest Fly Machine 3 sets · 12 reps · Light-moderate ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit with your back flat against the pad, elbows resting in the arm pads at chest height.
  2. Set a light-moderate weight.
  3. Slowly bring the pads/handles together in front of your chest in a hugging motion, keeping a slight bend in your elbows.
  4. Pause briefly, then slowly return to the start position — don't let the weight stack slam or let your arms drift too far back.
  5. Stop the range early if you feel any shoulder pinching, especially at the stretched-back position.
A supported, fixed-path way to train the chest without any of the shoulder stress of free-weight flys — the pads control the range so you can't overextend the shoulder joint. Pairs well with Chest Press for balanced upper body work.

❄️ Cool-Down 0/3
✓
Seated Hamstring Stretch 30 sec · Each leg ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit on the edge of a bench. Extend one leg straight in front.
  2. Keeping back straight (don't round!), hinge forward at the hips until you feel a stretch in the back of the thigh.
  3. Hold 30 seconds. Breathe deeply. Switch legs.
  4. If too intense, bend the knee slightly.
Stretching warm hamstrings after exercise is far more effective than cold stretching. Pays off in reduced back tightness the next day.

✓
Somatic Forward Fold 3–5 slow reps ▼
How to do it:
  1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, holding onto a counter or chair back for balance if needed.
  2. Slowly hinge forward at your hips, keeping a soft bend in the knees, letting your arms hang.
  3. Go only until you feel a gentle stretch in the back of your thighs — stop well before any pull in your lower back.
  4. From there, very slowly come back up — this slow, controlled "releasing" on the way up is the key part, not the fold itself.
  5. Pause standing tall, take a breath, then repeat for 3–5 slow reps. You may find you release a little further each time.
A "somatic" stretch works by slow, gentle contract-and-release rather than forcing a static hold — it calms the nervous system's guarding response instead of fighting against it, which is gentler on a sensitive back than a standard forward fold.

✓
90/90 Decompression 5–10 min ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back on the floor or a mat near a chair, bench, or your couch.
  2. Rest your calves up on the chair/bench so your hips and knees are both bent to roughly 90°.
  3. Let your lower back relax and flatten toward the floor — don't force it, just let gravity settle you into it.
  4. Arms can rest out to the sides or on your stomach. Breathe slowly and deeply.
  5. Stay here for 5–10 minutes, fully relaxed.
Unloads pressure off the discs and nerve roots without arching your back — the opposite motion of walking and standing, which compress the spine. A gentler, flexion-based alternative to devices like inversion tables, which tend to arch the lower back and can aggravate facet joints and narrowed nerve openings. Great to end on since you're already lying down.
