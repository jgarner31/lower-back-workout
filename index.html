🔥 Warm-Up 0/5
✓
Stationary Bike 10 min · Easy Pace ▼
How to do it:
  1. Adjust the seat so your knee has a slight bend at the bottom of each pedal stroke.
  2. Set resistance to a light level — you should be able to hold a conversation.
  3. Sit upright with a straight spine; avoid hunching forward.
  4. Pedal at a comfortable, steady pace for 10 minutes.
Loosens lower back joints, increases blood flow to the discs, and prepares your spine for movement without compression.

Cat-Cow Stretch 2 sets · 10 reps ▼
How to do it:
  1. Get on all fours — hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
  2. Cat: Exhale and round your spine up toward the ceiling, tucking your chin and pelvis.
  3. Cow: Inhale and drop your belly toward the floor, lifting your head and tailbone.
  4. Flow slowly between the two. Never force the range of motion. If kneeling bothers your knees, place a folded towel or mat pad under them.
One of the best mobilizers for arthritic lumbar joints. Move only as far as is pain-free.

✓
QL Ball Release 2–3 min · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Place a tennis ball or lacrosse ball on the floor.
  2. Lie on your back and position the ball just above your hip bone, slightly to one side of your spine — that is your QL muscle (Quadratus Lumborum).
  3. Let your body weight slowly sink into the ball. Stay relaxed — do not tense up or fight it.
  4. When you find a sore or tight spot, pause there for 20–30 seconds, breathing slowly and deeply.
  5. Gently shift the ball to new spots along the muscle. Spend 2–3 minutes per side. More tender = more work needed there.
The QL muscle connects your pelvis to your spine and is a hidden driver of lumbar arthritis pain. Tight QLs cause your back muscles to overwork and your spine to compress unevenly. Releasing them before exercise reduces baseline tightness and makes your Pallof Press and core work far more effective. Always release before stretching — not after.

✓
Deep Core Activation 2 sets · 10 reps ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor. Place your fingertips just inside your hip bones (the bony bumps at the front of your pelvis).
  2. Take a slow breath in through your nose.
  3. As you exhale slowly, gently draw your lower abdomen inward — as if bracing for a light poke in the stomach. Do NOT suck your stomach in hard or hold your breath.
  4. You should feel a subtle, deep tightening under your fingertips. That is your transverse abdominis — your deep core "corset" muscle — switching on.
  5. Hold the gentle brace for 5–6 seconds while continuing to breathe normally. Release fully. That is one rep. Do 10 reps.
Most back pain sufferers have a "turned off" deep core after months of guarding. This exercise wakes it back up. Your machine core work only works properly if this foundation is already activated. Takes 2 minutes and pays dividends the entire workout. Focus on the internal sensation — this is quiet, internal work, not a visible movement.

✓
Rowing Machine Workout A 5 min · Easy Pace ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit tall on the rower with feet strapped in and a light damper/resistance setting.
  2. Drive first with your legs, then lean back slightly and pull the handle to your lower ribs.
  3. Reverse the order on the way back: arms out, lean forward from the hips, then bend your knees.
  4. Keep your back straight the whole stroke — the power comes from your legs, not from yanking with your lower back.
  5. Row at an easy, conversational pace for 5 minutes.
A seated, low-impact full-body warm-up alternative to the bike that also primes your back and lats. Just be sure the drive comes from your legs and hips, not a rounded lower back.

✓
Ski Erg Workout B 5 min · Easy Pace ▼
How to do it:
  1. Stand facing the machine, feet shoulder-width apart, holding a handle in each hand at a comfortable overhead height — don't force a full overhead reach if your shoulder feels tight.
  2. Hinge gently at the hips and pull both handles down and back past your hips, like a poling motion in cross-country skiing.
  3. Let the handles return smoothly overhead and repeat.
  4. Keep the hip hinge light and your lower back neutral — don't round at the bottom of each pull.
  5. Work at an easy pace for 5 minutes.
Standing, low-impact, and works your lats, core, and legs together with zero floor work or spinal compression. Keep the overhead reach comfortable and the hip hinge gentle.

🧘 Mobility & Stretching 0/3
✓
Knee-to-Chest Stretch 30 sec · Each leg ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent.
  2. Slowly pull one knee toward your chest, clasping your hands behind the thigh.
  3. Hold 30 seconds — feel a gentle stretch in the lower back and glute.
  4. Switch legs. Then pull both knees to chest for 30 more seconds.
Gently opens the lumbar facet joints where arthritis and bone spurs develop, reducing morning stiffness.

✓
Figure-4 Piriformis Stretch 30 sec · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent.
  2. Cross your right ankle over your left knee (figure-4 shape).
  3. Reach through and pull your left thigh toward your chest until you feel a stretch in the right glute/hip.
  4. Hold 30 seconds, switch sides.
A tight piriformis can compress the sciatic nerve and worsen back pain. Often one of the most immediately relieving stretches.

✓
Hip Flexor Stretch (Kneeling) 30 sec · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Kneel on one knee on a mat (folded towel under knee — use extra padding if your knees are sensitive).
  2. Place other foot flat on floor in front, making a 90° angle.
  3. Push hips forward gently until you feel a stretch in the front of the back hip.
  4. Hold 30 seconds. Keep spine upright — don't arch your lower back. Switch sides.
  5. Knee-friendly alternative: Stand and place your back foot up on a bench behind you instead of kneeling, then gently press hips forward.
Tight hip flexors pull the pelvis forward and increase lumbar compression. Stretching them daily is one of the highest-impact things you can do. Use the standing version if kneeling irritates your knees.

💪 Core & Stability (Machine-Based) 0/3
✓
Seated Ab Crunch Machine 3 sets · 12–15 reps · Light-moderate ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit in the machine with the pad against your upper chest/shoulders and feet flat on the floor or footrests.
  2. Set a light-to-moderate weight — this is about controlled range of motion, not maxing out.
  3. Exhale and curl your torso forward against the pad, contracting your abs.
  4. Slowly return to the start position without letting the weight stack slam down.
  5. Keep the motion smooth and controlled — no jerking or bouncing.
Gives you the crunch stimulus without kneeling on the floor or straining your neck like a floor crunch. The seat supports your spine so the movement stays controlled and safe for an arthritic back.

✓
Machine Back Extension 3 sets · 12 reps · Light weight ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit in the machine with your lower back against the pad, thighs secured under the leg pads.
  2. Set a light weight to start.
  3. Slowly lean back, extending through your hips and lower back within a comfortable, pain-free range.
  4. Squeeze gently at the end range — do NOT hyperextend or arch aggressively.
  5. Return slowly and under control to the start position.
Strengthens the erector spinae (the muscles supporting your lumbar spine) in a fully supported, seated position — no floor hyperextension bench required, and the machine limits the range so you can't overarch.

✓
Pallof Press 2–3 sets · 10–12 reps · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. At a cable machine, set a D-handle at chest height.
  2. Stand sideways to the machine, feet shoulder-width apart. Grab the handle with both hands and hold it at your chest, close to your sternum.
  3. Step far enough away from the machine that there is light tension on the cable.
  4. Brace your deep core (like you practiced in Deep Core Activation). Keep your hips square — do not let the cable pull you sideways or rotate your torso.
  5. Slowly press your hands straight out in front of you until your arms are fully extended. Pause 1–2 seconds — feel your core resisting the rotation.
  6. Slowly return your hands to your chest. That is one rep. Complete all reps on one side, then turn around and repeat facing the other direction.
Trains your core to resist rotation, standing, with no floor and no shoulder overhead loading — ideal given your shoulder arthritis. Protects your lumbar spine during everyday twisting (turning to reverse a car, reaching sideways, getting out of bed). Start very light — the challenge is staying perfectly still, not the weight.

🦵 Lower Body Strength (Spine-Friendly) 0/4
✓
Leg Press Machine 3 sets · 12 reps · Light weight ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit in the machine with back fully supported against the pad.
  2. Place feet shoulder-width apart on the platform.
  3. Lower platform until knees are around 90° — don't let knees cave inward.
  4. Push through heels to extend (don't lock out knees at the top).
  5. Start with very light weight — not a max-effort exercise.
Builds quads, glutes, and hamstrings WITHOUT loading your spine — unlike squats or deadlifts. The safest lower body machine for back conditions.

How to do it:
  1. Sit in the machine so the back of your ankles rest on the lower pad.

Tight, weak hamstrings rotate the pelvis and increase disc and facet joint stress. The seated version is spine-neutral unlike the lying version.

Hip Abductor Machine Workout A 3 sets · 15 reps · Light weight ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit in the machine with pads on the outside of your knees.
  2. Sit tall, back against pad, core lightly braced.
  3. Press knees outward against pads in a controlled motion.

Hip abductors stabilize your pelvis. Weak abductors cause the pelvis to drop when walking, transferring stress directly to the lower back.

Leg Extension Machine Workout A 3 sets · 12 reps · Light-moderate ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit in the machine with your knees aligned to the machine's pivot point and shins behind the lower pad.
  2. Adjust the back pad so your knees sit right at the edge of the seat.
  3. Extend your legs to lift the pad until legs are nearly straight — don't violently lock out.
  4. Pause briefly at the top, then lower slowly and under control.
Isolates the quads with zero spinal loading — fully seated and supported. Pairs well with Leg Press. Keep the weight light-moderate and ease off the top range if you feel any knee discomfort.

Hip Adductor Machine Workout B 3 sets · 15 reps · Light weight ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit in the adductor machine with pads on the inside of your knees.
  2. Sit upright, back against pad, feet on footrests.
  3. Squeeze knees inward together against the pads in a controlled motion.
  4. Slowly return. Keep core gently engaged throughout.
Inner thigh strength balances the hip abductors and keeps the pelvis properly aligned, reducing asymmetric loading on your lumbar spine.

✓
Suitcase Carry Workout B 3 sets · 30–40 sec · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Hold a single dumbbell or kettlebell at your side, like a suitcase, feet hip-width apart.
  2. Gently brace your core and stand tall — do not lean or side-bend toward the weight.
  3. Walk 20–30 steps (about 30–40 seconds), keeping your torso upright and level the whole time.
  4. Switch the weight to your other hand and repeat.
An excellent anti-lateral-flexion core exercise — your obliques and deep trunk muscles work hard to keep you upright while you walk, with zero spinal flexion, rotation, or floor work. Start light; the goal is a level torso, not how much weight you can carry.

🏋️ Upper Body (Spinal Decompression) 0/3
✓
Lat Pulldown 3 sets · 12 reps · Moderate weight ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit at the lat pulldown machine, thighs secured under pads.
  2. Grip bar slightly wider than shoulder-width, palms facing away.
  3. Lean back slightly (~15°) and pull bar down to upper chest.
  4. Squeeze shoulder blades together at bottom. Slowly return to full arm extension.
  5. Do NOT yank or use momentum — slow and controlled. Stop short of full stretch if your shoulder feels any pinching.
Pulling exercises gently decompress the lumbar spine while building upper back muscles that support posture — a double benefit for back pain sufferers.

✓
Seated Cable Row 3 sets · 12 reps · Moderate weight ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit at cable row station with feet on footrests, knees slightly bent.
  2. Sit tall — back upright, chest forward. Grip the handle.
  3. Pull handle toward lower chest/belly button while squeezing shoulder blades together.
  4. Hold 1 second. Slowly return to full arm extension without rounding your back.
  5. Avoid rocking forward and back to generate momentum.
Strengthens the mid-back which pulls shoulders back and reduces the forward-rounding posture that compresses lumbar discs all day.

✓
Chest Press Machine Workout A 3 sets · 12 reps · Light-moderate ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit with your back flat against the pad, handles at chest height.
  2. Set a light-moderate weight.
  3. Press the handles forward until your arms are extended but not locked out.
  4. Slowly return to start, keeping shoulder blades lightly retracted — don't let shoulders roll forward at the bottom.
  5. Stop the movement early if you feel any shoulder pinching.
A machine chest press keeps the movement path fixed and supported, unlike a free-weight bench press — much easier on an arthritic shoulder since there's nothing to stabilize. Keep the weight light and stop the range before any pinch or ache.

✓
Standing Single-Arm Cable Row Workout B 3 sets · 10–12 reps · Each side ▼
How to do it:
  1. Set a D-handle at chest height on a cable stack.
  2. Stand side-on to the machine, feet shoulder-width apart, knees soft.
  3. Grab the handle with the arm farther from the machine.
  4. Brace your core — resist the urge to let the cable rotate your torso.
  5. Pull the handle to your ribs, squeezing your shoulder blade back, then slowly extend back out while staying square.
  6. Complete all reps, then turn around and repeat with the other arm.
Does double duty — a back/pulling exercise AND an anti-rotation core exercise, since you have to actively resist the cable twisting your spine. Great way to get back strength and core stability together without extra floor work.

❄️ Cool-Down 0/2
✓
Seated Hamstring Stretch 30 sec · Each leg ▼
How to do it:
  1. Sit on the edge of a bench. Extend one leg straight in front.
  2. Keeping back straight (don't round!), hinge forward at the hips until you feel a stretch in the back of the thigh.
  3. Hold 30 seconds. Breathe deeply. Switch legs.
  4. If too intense, bend the knee slightly.
Stretching warm hamstrings after exercise is far more effective than cold stretching. Pays off in reduced back tightness the next day.

✓
Child's Pose 2 sets · 60 sec ▼
How to do it:
  1. Kneel on a mat, big toes together, knees apart (use a folded towel under the knees if needed).
  2. Sit hips back toward heels as far as comfortably possible.
  3. Reach arms forward on the floor, let forehead rest on the mat.
  4. Breathe deeply into your lower back. Feel the gentle stretch release with each breath.
  5. If knees are uncomfortable even with padding, do a seated forward fold in a chair instead: sit tall, then hinge forward reaching toward your shins.
Gently decompresses the lumbar vertebrae. One of the most universally relieving finishers for lower back conditions. Never skip this one — use the seated alternative if your knees need it.
